Digital seller guide

How to use the Digital Product Profit Calculator

Digital products can look almost pure-profit, but marketplace fees, payment processing, listing fees, ad cost, software subscriptions, and customer support still matter. Use this monthly model to estimate whether a digital product line is worth maintaining.

  1. Enter the average price per digital product sale.
  2. Add expected monthly sales for that product or product group.
  3. Enter marketplace fees, processing fees, listing fees, and ad cost per sale.
  4. Add monthly software costs such as design tools, mockup tools, or keyword tools.
  5. Review monthly profit and margin before scaling ads or creating more products.

Digital product profit formula

Monthly revenue = Price per sale x Monthly sales

Per-sale costs = Marketplace fees + Processing fees + Listing fee + Ad cost + Support cost

Total monthly costs = Per-sale costs x Monthly sales + Monthly software cost

Monthly profit = Monthly revenue - Total monthly costs

The monthly view is useful for digital sellers because software, design assets, mockup tools, and keyword tools are recurring costs even when fulfillment is automatic.

Example digital product calculation

If a planner sells for $7.99 and gets 85 sales per month, monthly revenue is $679.15. After marketplace fees, processing, listing fees, ad cost, support allowance, and $29 software cost, the calculator estimates how much is left as monthly profit.

Common digital product mistakes

  • Calling digital products free to sell while ignoring software subscriptions.
  • Underpricing templates because there is no shipping cost.
  • Forgetting that small fixed payment fees matter on low-ticket downloads.
  • Scaling ads without knowing profit per sale.

Digital product profit FAQ

What digital products can I calculate?

You can model printable planners, SVG bundles, Canva templates, Notion templates, spreadsheets, mockups, presets, fonts, and similar downloadable products.

Should I include my time?

For a simple monthly model, include ongoing support time as support cost. For product creation time, track it separately as an upfront investment.

Why is monthly profit better than one-sale profit?

Monthly profit includes recurring software and ad costs, which are easy to miss when you only look at one transaction.
